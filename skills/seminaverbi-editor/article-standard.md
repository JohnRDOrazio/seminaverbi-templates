# What a SeminaVerbi article is

Semina Verbi ("Seeds of the Word") examines how the Gospel and the Word of God have influenced human
culture, and how cultural and creative works carry seeds of truth related to the Word. An article about a
work (book, film, TV series, album, song, play, artwork) or a person is **not a copy of Wikipedia**.

## Focus

- How the storyline, characters and message are shaped by, or aligned with, the Gospel: love, forgiveness,
  faith, hope, virtue, redemption, conversion.
- Bibliographic and commercial detail (editions, sales, box office) only as far as it identifies the work:
  the infobox (`SV infobox work` / `SV infobox person`) is usually enough.
- Distinguish genuine alignment with the Gospel from biblical imagery used for entertainment or
  curiosity. Such works can still be covered; say which it is.

## Scripture

- Name and **quote every Bible verse** the work references or alludes to, with
  `{{SV Bible quote|ref=…}}` (book names in the wiki's language). Do not paraphrase a verse instead of
  quoting it.

## Discernment

- Read the work critically for seeds of true faith **and** for expressions of errors: Arianism, Gnosticism,
  Pelagianism, Nestorianism, Monophysitism, Docetism, Donatism, Marcionism, Modernism, New Age, woke
  ideology. Point at the concrete scene or lyric and at the teaching it departs from; never apply labels
  loosely.
- End the article with the section started by `{{SV discernment}}` (same heading on every wiki), then
  `{{SV references}}`. `{{SV references}}` writes its own heading (Notes, Note, Notas…): never put a
  heading of your own above it, or the page shows an empty section.

## One work, one page, one field

Each work has its own page: a novel and its film adaptation are two articles that link to each other,
each filed in its own field (one field category per page: the work's medium or form, or a person's main
domain). The subject of a work is carried by the Gospel themes, the links and the infobox.

## Shape of an article

1. Infobox, lead paragraph.
2. Sections on the story / content as it bears on the Gospel, with Bible quotes where the work points to
   Scripture, and `{{SV quote}}` for other quotations (Fathers, Magisterium, the author of the work).
3. `{{SV discernment}}` section.
4. See also (with `{{SV cathopedia}}` outside it.wiki), `{{SV references}}`, categories: exactly one
   field category and the Gospel themes the text really explores (`categories.md`), chosen
   with the Jev routine in `SKILL.md`.
5. `{{SV WIP|user=…|date=…}}` at the top while the article is incomplete.
